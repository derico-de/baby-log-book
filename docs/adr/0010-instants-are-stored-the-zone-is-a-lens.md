# Instants are stored, the zone is a lens, and the hour travels

Every time the app records is a **UTC instant**, carried alongside the IANA zone id the recording Device was in — `Europe/Berlin`, never the numeric offset `+02:00`. Every time the app *shows* is that instant projected through a zone at display time. Which zone depends on what the projection is for, and the two consumers deliberately differ: **days are cut in the [Household Zone](../../CONTEXT.md), while the app's appearance follows each Device's own clock.** What both share is the [Day Start](../../CONTEXT.md) hour — the number travels even though the zone does not.

We rejected storing a bare instant, which is what most logs do, because the recording zone is unrecoverable once it has not been written: a v2 that wants to draw a fortnight abroad in the time it actually happened would have no data to draw it from, for every Entry ever logged. We rejected storing a numeric offset instead of a zone id because an offset is a dead number — it renders one wall time back and cannot say what `05:00` means in that place on any other date, so it can never re-derive a day boundary. And we rejected letting each Device or each Entry supply the bucketing lens, because [ADR-0006](0006-targets-are-stated-not-learned.md)'s sibling decision made Day Start per Household for one reason — otherwise "yesterday" means two things inside one Household — and that reason does not care whether the split comes from two babies or two continents.

## Consequences

- **The rule that keeps all of this true, and the one a future implementer could break silently:** anything used for ordering, merging, the pull cursor or a duration is an **instant**; local wall time is a display-time projection and never enters a comparison. Parse a wall-time string into the merge key and the bug appears twice a year and on every flight.
- **A zone change is not clock skew.** [ADR-0004](0004-cursor-is-not-the-merge-key.md)'s one-sided sanity window fires on a wrong clock, and a phone stepping from Berlin to New York does not move `Date.now()` by a millisecond. Neither does a DST transition. The guard needs no zone awareness at all — which is only true while the rule above holds.
- **The Recording Zone is captured and nothing in v1 reads it.** That is the point: it is insurance against an unrecoverable loss, on the same argument that pulled Milestones into v1. It is also what lets the travel refinement ship later without a migration over the whole log. A [Revision](../../CONTEXT.md) never rewrites it, so correcting a German Feed from Bucharest does not restamp it.
- **The Household Zone is one value, not a history**, and changing it re-buckets the past exactly as changing the Day Start already does — the settings screen warns before saving. It is **suggested, never applied**: an Owner's Device reporting a different zone on every sync for 48 hours prompts once, dismissibly, and never again for that zone. A layover must not move a Household, and the app does not nag.
- **A family abroad for a fortnight chooses**: change the Household Zone on arrival and change it back, or accept days cut at 23:00 local. A Member reading from another country sees the Household's days, because they are reading about a Baby who lives somewhere.
- **Rejected as the apparent best of both**: bucketing by the Household Zone while printing each Entry's clock face in its own Recording Zone. Printed times inside one day group stop being monotonic — `20:00` followed by `03:00` in the same bucket.
- **[ADR-0008](0008-appearance-follows-the-clock.md)'s open question is closed, and the answer is divergence.** The appearance resolver reads the Device's clock because it answers *is it dark in the room I am standing in*, and because it must run before first paint with no replica necessarily synced. Coupling it to the Household Zone would put a white screen in a dark bedroom in Bucharest at 23:30 — the precise failure that ADR exists to prevent. So the resolver takes the Day Start as a plain number and applies it to the Device's local clock.
- **DST has a defined answer rather than an accidental one.** A Day Start whose nominal hour is skipped resolves to the instant the clock jumps to; one that happens twice takes the first occurrence, so the boundary stays monotone. **Every duration is elapsed real time**, so a Sleep from 23:00 to 07:00 across spring-forward is seven hours — the Baby slept seven hours. At a 05:00 Day Start against 02:00–03:00 transitions the resolution rule almost never fires; it exists so a Household that sets 03:00 is not undefined.
- **The [Export](../../CONTEXT.md) carries both halves and the lens.** Timestamps go out as one ISO-8601 column with its offset plus an `occurred_at_zone` column with the IANA id, and the zip gains a small `household.csv` holding the Household Zone and the Day Start hour. A day bucket is derived and never stored, so an export without the lens is a pile of numbers whose meaning has been left behind — which would make [ADR-0007](0007-export-is-an-escape-hatch.md)'s escape hatch open onto nothing.
