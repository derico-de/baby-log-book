# The Night Period ends at the Day Start

A Household may state a **Night Period** — one hour, the hour its night begins. Its end is the **Day Start**, the boundary that already cuts the Household's days. A Feed whose Feed Interval would come due inside that period comes due at the Day Start instead: fed at 21:00 on a three-hour interval, the next Feed is due at 07:00 rather than at midnight.

The Feed Interval is stated as a single duration and repeats around the clock ([ADR-0006](0006-targets-are-stated-not-learned.md)), which is right for a day and wrong for a night. Nobody keeps to three-hourly at 1am on purpose; they feed when she wakes and go back to bed. Before this, the sticky header spent the night printing a Feed that was two hours overdue, and — since [ADR-0031](0031-a-notice-is-a-target-reached-and-the-household-states-the-offset.md) — the deployment pushed that overdue Feed to a sleeping phone. Both were the app being confidently wrong about an hour it knows nothing about.

## Consequences

- **One hour, not two.** The Household states when the night *begins*; it ends at the Day Start. Spec §7.2 already turns on that boundary settling both ends of the night — it is what makes a Sleep a Night Sleep — and a second end hour could drift out of step with it, leaving a Household whose night ends at 07:00 for feeds and 05:00 for sleeps. A Household that wants its night to end later moves its Day Start, which is the same fact said once.
- **Feeds only.** A Wake Window is *how long she is comfortably awake* and says nothing about the hour; a Bottle Life is how long milk stays good, and milk does not keep longer because it is dark. Both would be a different claim, so `dueInstant` is unchanged and only `feedDueInstant` passes through the night.
- **It moves an instant, and only forward.** The whole feature is `pastNight`, in `$domain/time`: an instant whose clock face falls inside the Night becomes the next Day Start, and every other instant is returned untouched. So the header, the notifier and anything after them keep the promise ADR-0006 made and [ADR-0031](0031-a-notice-is-a-target-reached-and-the-household-states-the-offset.md) kept — one due-instant computation, two callers, no chance of disagreeing.
- **A Feed logged in the night still re-anchors.** The night moves the due instant, never the anchor. She wakes at 03:00 and is fed, and the next Feed is due at the Day Start — not three hours later at 06:00, and not at the Day Start of the night before.
- **Nothing changes until a Parent states an hour.** The column arrives NULL and NULL means *this Household keeps no Night Period*, so every existing Household reads exactly as it did. An hour the migration invented would be a guess, and not knowing when this Household's night is is the entire reason the setting exists. Clearing the field states it again: like a Notice Offset, this is a field whose `null` is a value a Member chose, so both storage edges carry a presence flag rather than a COALESCE.
- **It is a Household setting, not a Device one.** The server holds the notifier's timer and cannot read a Device Setting, and when a household's night is, is a fact about the routine — the same answer on Oma's phone as on mum's. So it travels as a `household` Revision like the Day Start, and a Parent can set it from a phone that is offline.
- **A Night that begins at the hour it ends is no Night.** `nightPeriodOf` returns null for it rather than treating it as a Night that swallows the day. It is the one degenerate pair the two hours can make, and reading it as *off* is the only answer that cannot silently stop a Household's Feed Interval altogether.
