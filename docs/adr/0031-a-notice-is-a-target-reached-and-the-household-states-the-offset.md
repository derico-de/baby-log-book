# A Notice is a Target reached, and the Household states the offset

The deployment sends three kinds of push: a **bottle nearly out**, a **Feed due**, and a **Wake Window up**. Each is one of the Household's own Targets coming round — nothing else notifies, and no Notice carries a number the app decided. The two new ones are said at an **offset the Household states**: the Feed Notice a stated number of seconds *before* the Feed Interval is up, the Sleep Notice a stated number *after* the Wake Window is up. Either offset can be set to *never*.

[ADR-0030](0030-a-push-is-the-bottle-chime-reaching-a-sleeping-phone.md) shipped exactly one push and wrote "nothing else notifies — not an overdue Feed, not a Wake Window". This supersedes that sentence and nothing else in it. The reason it held then was that the Bottle Chime already existed as a Device Setting and the push was that reminder reaching a locked screen; a Feed Notice had no such half. What changed is that a Household running the app at 3am is looking at the same sticky header the chime came from, and the two figures beside the bottle countdown — *next feed in 40m*, *awake 2h10* — are the ones people actually miss. They are already computed, from Targets already stated, by folds already written; the whole feature is delivery.

## Consequences

- **One fold decides all three.** `liveNotices` in `$domain/notices` is the only thing that knows what is due; the notifier imports it and does delivery. It composes `dueInstant`, `anchorEntry` and `bottlesNearingEnd` rather than restating them, so the sticky header and the notifier can only ever agree — the promise [ADR-0006](0006-targets-are-stated-not-learned.md) made when it put the due-instant computation in one module, kept for a second caller.
- **Every Notice is anchored to an Entry, and the kind is part of the key.** "Said once per Device" needs no clock and survives a restart: the bottle, the previous Feed, the last Sleep. Two Notices can hang on the *same* Entry at the same moment — a bottle nearly out is also the Feed the next interval will be measured from — so `push_sent` gained a `kind` column; without it one would have silenced the other. Logging the next Feed re-anchors the Notice, which is how the reminder comes round again.
- **Every Notice carries an `until`, and that is the fix for a late one.** A push is queued by somebody else's service and handed over whenever the phone next surfaces. A bottle Notice's `until` is the bottle's own due instant — the moment the server ends the Feed ([ADR-0017](0017-a-bottle-past-its-life-ends-its-own-feed.md)) — so it is asked for with a TTL of *exactly what is left* rather than a flat quarter of an hour, and a push service holding it past that drops it instead of popping *bottle nearly out* over a Feed that ended twenty minutes ago. The worker re-checks `until` before showing anything, because *should* is not a guarantee anybody else is keeping. That check can spend the app's `userVisibleOnly` promise, which is the honest trade: it is reachable only by a service that overran a TTL it was given, and a notification that is no longer true is worse than one that never arrives.
- **A stopped Feed is silent, at every layer.** The plan is re-read from the log on every thirty-second tick and a stopped bottle is not in it; the TTL keeps a queued push from outliving the bottle; the worker's deadline catches a service that ignored the TTL. What is left is the Member who stops a Feed on a phone that is offline — the server cannot know, and no design can make it know.
- **A Notice has a fifteen-minute window.** A Feed that is due stays due and a Baby past her Wake Window stays past it, so without a window the notifier would repeat itself forever — the once-per record is pruned by age. The arithmetic says it instead: outside the window there is no Notice to say. A quarter of an hour, and not a setting; what a Household has an opinion about is when the window *opens*.
- **Neither Notice interrupts what it is about.** No Feed Notice while a Feed is running, and none while she is asleep — a Wake Window is time not covered by a Sleep (spec §8.5), so it does not apply. *A feed is due* said to the person feeding her is noise.
- **The offsets are Household settings, and the Bottle Chime's lead is still not one.** [ADR-0029](0029-the-bottle-chime-is-a-device-setting.md) put the chime's ten minutes beyond reach because it is not an interval anyone keeps to; that stands, and the bottle Notice is unchanged. The two new offsets are different: the server holds the timer and cannot read a Device Setting, and *how much warning before a feed* is a fact about the routine — the same answer on Oma's phone as on mum's. So they travel as `household` Revisions like the Day Start, a Parent sets them from a phone that is offline, and the notifier learns them from the log rather than from a side channel. **Whether a phone is woken at all stays exactly where ADR-0030 put it**: in the existence of a subscription, which is the Bottle Chime switch.
- **Offered as a short list, not a free number.** Never, on the dot, or five to sixty minutes. Nobody has a fifty-three-minute opinion about how much warning is useful, and a select is one thumb.
- **Zero on every existing Household.** The migration defaults both offsets to the due instant itself, so a Household that already switched the chime on starts getting the two new Notices. An offset the app invented would be a guess; zero is the only value that is not one.
- **The text says the offset out loud.** *Due in 10 minutes* and *due now* are different facts, and *awake 30 minutes past her window* and *her window is up* are two more, so each gets its own sentence rather than one that quietly rounds. Composed on the server in the Member's own language, like every other Notice — the worker has no locale of its own.
