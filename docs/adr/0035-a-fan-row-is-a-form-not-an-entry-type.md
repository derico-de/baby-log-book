# A fan row is a form, not an entry type

The fan was described as *one row per entry type* — in `Fan.svelte`, in spec §8.5, and in the test that asserted it. It never was. There are **eight** entry types and **six** rows: `breast_feed`, `bottle_feed` and `meal` all sit behind the single *Feed* row, told apart by a segmented control inside one sheet. [ADR-0028](0028-a-nappy-is-one-form.md) then folded two rows into one from the other direction, so pee and poop became fields rather than rows.

So the rule the fan has actually been following is: **a row is a form.** A new entry type must justify a new *form* before it may claim a row, and folding into a form that already exists costs no row at all. Two entry types arrived in a fortnight — Tummy time and the Pee & poop rework — at a net cost of one row.

This matters because the fan is the app's **only** create-from-nothing surface. Every sheet is mounted in `src/routes/+page.svelte` and nothing else opens one; the two exceptions (*She's awake*, *Feed while asleep* on a timeline row) act on a Sleep that already exists. So there is no admission test to write and never was — admission is automatic, which is why *"what earns a fan slot"* went unanswered twice ([issue 18](../../.scratch/baby-log-book/issues/18-milestones-entry-type.md) noted that frequency is not the test, but only because there was room). The question was unanswerable as posed. The obligation sits on the form, not on the row.

## Consequences

- **The forcing function the ticket feared is not real.** [Issue 27](../../.scratch/baby-log-book/issues/27-the-fan-is-out-of-room.md) argued the next entry type decides the fan's shape by force. It does not: the next type's first obligation is to fold, and every candidate it named — health events, medication, temperature — is out of scope anyway.
- **Measurement and Milestone stay in the fan.** The corollary is that a row leaves only by being given another create surface, and there is none. [Issue 18](../../.scratch/baby-log-book/issues/18-milestones-entry-type.md)'s refusal to invent a baby-profile screen for a monthly action holds rather than being reversed quietly.
- **The ceiling is stated and tested, at eight rows.** Measured in Chrome at 360×780 — a Galaxy S22, installed, the smallest screen the fan is known to work on: `.head` ends at y=194, the bottom row at y=704, so `n·54 + (n−1)·8 ≤ 510` gives eight with 22px to spare. jsdom does no layout, so the assertion in `render.test.ts` is arithmetic over the fan's own constants rather than a measurement — which is what makes it a trip-wire that goes red when a *row* is added rather than a snapshot that goes red when a pixel moves.
- **The room was bought twice over, and neither was the restructure.** The ticket recommended a two-column grid of tiles, whose own stated cost was that the fan "stops being a menu and becomes a pad". It was not needed. The fan now **curves around the FAB** instead of stacking above it — the row at the thumb sits beside the button, the rows above swing back to the right edge on a `cos(t·π/2)` arc — which reclaims the 74px the stack spent clearing the FAB. And Pico's inherited `button { margin-bottom: 16px }`, the trap `DESIGN.md` already warns about, was costing **80px of dead space** across six rows in the one control short of room. Together: six rows to eight, with the pill, its label, its sub-line and its type colour all untouched.
- **The rows share one width.** With auto widths both edges go ragged and six pills read as a scatter rather than a curve, so the fan is `align-items: stretch`. The widest string in three languages is German's *der Schlaf läuft weiter* at a 250px pill, which leaves the bottom row 18px clear of the left edge; the `max-width` and ellipsis on the label are a safety net that no current string reaches.
- **A true circle was rejected for a cosine.** `sqrt(1 − t²)` leaves the top row snapping back 44px while its neighbours move 15, and the kink reads as a mistake rather than a curve.
