# A Rescue Link is minted from Settings

A **Rescue Link** stops being a terminal-only artifact. Any Member mints one for **themselves** from Settings — that is the *add my tablet* flow, and minting for yourself is no power you did not already hold — and a **Parent** mints one for **any Member**: Oma's new phone, and the Hub whose re-auth flow asks for exactly this ([ADR-0034](0034-a-hub-is-a-device-and-its-reads-are-derived.md)). [ADR-0005](0005-claim-links-instead-of-passwords.md) said a Rescue Link is minted "from inside the container"; this supersedes that one consequence and nothing else — the claim mechanics, the single use, the POST-behind-a-button all stand. The pressure that forced it is the Hub against a public origin: `babylog rescue` under `docker exec` reaches a self-hoster, and a hosted family's only alternative was a fresh Invite, which claims a *second* Member and splits attribution forever. But the hole was never Hub-shaped — a hosted family's lost phone fell through it too, and the Rescue Link was already, quietly, the only way the same person gets a second Device at all.

The genuinely new power is named rather than hidden: **a Parent can now bind a Device that writes as another Member.** An Invite never granted that — it creates someone new; a rescue writes into an existing Member's attribution. The counterweight is visibility: an unclaimed Rescue sits in the same pending list as unclaimed Invites, named for the Member it re-binds, revocable by any Parent, and recording who minted it.

## Consequences

- **A Rescue Link means *bind this Member to another Device* — and says nothing about the old ones.** Claiming one leaves every existing session standing, because the claim cannot know whether it is the lost phone or the new tablet, and auto-revoking would sign the phone out for adding the tablet. The stolen phone still wants its one session ended; today the only remote act is removing the whole Member, and a per-Device revoke is deliberately **not** built here — it is its own feature, not a claim-link flag.
- **One TTL, 60 minutes, everywhere — CLI included.** Fifteen minutes was priced for an operator standing at the terminal; a Settings mint travels over WhatsApp to someone fumbling with a new phone. Sixty minutes covers a live phone call and stays two orders of magnitude short of an Invite's 7 days. A TTL that depended on where the mint happened would be a distinction nothing else in the model makes.
- **Removal burns the Member's pending Rescue Links and Removal still ends everything.** A rescue claimed for a Removed Member must fail — otherwise removing a Member races their unclaimed rescue. And because `revokeMember` kills every session the Member has, a credential thief who minted themselves a second Device gains no persistence that removal does not end.
- **ADR-0034's blast-radius sentence narrows from Device to Member.** "Cannot open the door to another Device" was written when no session could mint any link; a stolen hub credential can now self-rescue onto a second box. What it still cannot do is open the door to another *Member* — and removal ends the original session, the rescued one, and the pending link alike, so the control ADR-0034 leans on (revocation, not expiry) is unchanged.
- **The sole signed-in-nobody case keeps the terminal.** A sole Parent's lost phone leaves no session to mint from, by construction — no in-app design can reach it. `babylog rescue` remains, and for a hosted Household that means the operator, on request.
- **CONTEXT.md's Rescue Link entry is rewritten** — it no longer says "minted from the server itself", and it owns both of its meanings, recovery and addition, explicitly.
