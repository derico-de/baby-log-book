# A Bottle records the Intake

A Bottle Feed stores one amount: the **Intake** — what she drank, held in `volume_ml`. While the bottle is still in play the field carries the best current statement of it (the full bottle just poured); when some comes back, entering the leftover **subtracts it from the Intake in place** and is not kept. This supersedes [ADR-0015](0015-a-bottle-records-what-was-offered-and-what-came-back.md) and chooses exactly the alternative it rejected, because living with the pair showed the offered figure was primary nowhere: stats, the timeline and the export all wanted the intake, and the sheet was holding one number in the field while meaning another in a note underneath. Two personas forced the issue — one derives the intake (water-measure presets, then a leftover), one measures or calculates it and just types it — and both must end at the same authoritative field, or the log means different things depending on who fed her.

## Consequences

- **`volume_ml` changes meaning, not shape.** Every new write stores the Intake there and never sets `leftover_ml`. The old field stays in the payload type, frozen, so entries written before the change still parse.
- **One reading lens covers both eras.** The derivation ADR-0015 introduced (`volume − leftover`, clamped at zero, null leftover reads as the whole bottle) becomes the *reading rule for legacy rows*: for a new row the leftover is null and the lens passes the Intake straight through. Stats, the timeline and the export all read through it, so the numbers do not move.
- **No migration and no `PROTOCOL_VERSION` bump.** An old client's write (offered + leftover) reads correctly on a new client through the lens; a new client's write (intake, null leftover) reads correctly on an old client as a bottle with nothing said about a leftover. Neither direction produces a wrong record, which is the only thing the version number is for (spec §5.5).
- **Editing a legacy row converts it.** The edit sheet shows the derived Intake as the amount; saving an amount change writes the new Intake and explicitly nulls the leftover — an ordinary field write under last-write-wins, visible in the revision history like any correction. Untouched legacy rows keep their pair forever.
- **The leftover affordance applies once and clears.** Entering a leftover subtracts it from the Intake field, then empties itself; leaving it filled would invite a second application and a double subtraction. A mistaken subtraction is corrected the way every number here is corrected — by editing the field. Clamped at zero, never validated.
- **The timeline speaks one figure.** The `130 ml of 170` form is retired; a row shows its Intake, on legacy and new rows alike. What was offered on a legacy row is still in its stored payload and its history, not in the reading.
- **The export carries `intake_ml` and retires `volume_ml`, `leftover_ml` and `taken_ml`.** One column, same value the app shows, for every era. This is an accepted loss: a legacy row's offered/leftover pair no longer reaches the file. The revision log keeps them permanently ([ADR-0002](0002-append-only-revisions.md)), and the file is an escape hatch for a person with a spreadsheet ([ADR-0007](0007-export-is-an-escape-hatch.md)), not an archival format.
- **Formula presets speak water and write milk.** When the contents is formula, the quick amounts are the water measures a parent actually prepares — 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210, 240, 270 — each labelled with the final volume it yields (→70, →100, →135, →170, →200, →235, →270, →305), and tapping one writes that **final** volume into the Intake field. The table is hardcoded: the yields are empirical per-brand scoop arithmetic, not a factor, and the free field is the escape for a brand that disagrees. Breast-milk bottles keep the plain presets — what is poured is what is drinkable.
- **ADR-0015's concurrency worry is accepted as ordinary.** Two Members correcting one field at once resolve by last-write-wins like every other field in the app; the pair bought no extra safety there that the visible revision history does not already provide.
