# The Bottle Chime is a Device Setting

Ten minutes before a started bottle's Life runs out, a Device that has been switched on for it plays **two soft notes**. Nothing else happens: no badge, no banner, no second colour on the row, no Entry, nothing written and nothing synced. The chime exists for one thing the countdown alone cannot do — a bottle put down at 3am is a bottle nobody is looking at, and the point of knowing it is nearly out is to *offer the rest while there is still time*. Ten minutes is enough to pick her up again; the row goes on printing the exact figure for anyone who looks.

We chose a **Device Setting**, off until switched on, over a Household setting and over a fourth Target. The rule in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md) decides it: a Device Setting answers a question about *this phone*, and whether a sound is wanted is exactly that — the phone in the room with the baby is not the phone on a sleeping partner's bedside table, and it is not Oma's handbag on a Tuesday when she is not sitting. The same bottle is open on every Device in the Household; a Household-wide flag would make all of them ring for it.

## Consequences

- **It is the first noise the app has ever made, and it is not a Target.** [ADR-0016](0016-the-bottle-life-is-a-target-not-a-verdict.md) held the line that reaching the number is not an event the app has an opinion about, and the vocabulary keeps *alarm* and *reminder* away from **Target** deliberately. The chime does not move that line: it says nothing about the milk, it does not change what the row prints, and it is silent on every Device that has not asked. What it asserts is only *there is an open bottle with ten minutes left on the number you typed* — which is the same claim the row was already making, addressed to someone who is not looking at it.
- **The lead is fixed at ten minutes and is not a fifth setting.** It is not an interval anyone is keeping to; it is how much warning is useful, and a number nobody would have an opinion about until they had one is not worth a control. It is clamped to **half the Bottle Life**, so a Household that has typed twenty minutes hears *nearly out* rather than a chime as the feed begins.
- **It fires once per bottle, and the record is in memory.** The Set holds ids of *open* bottles only, so it cannot grow with the log, and nothing about it reaches the replica or the sync log. A Device relaunched inside the window chimes again — which is right: someone has just picked that phone up.
- **Only while the app is open on that phone.** There is no timer in a worker and no notification. A browser will not let a page make noise before someone has touched it, so the shell primes the audio context on the first pointer down and again when the setting is switched on; a phone that is locked, or has the app closed, stays silent. This is the honest half of the feature and Settings says so. The real answer for a closed app is v2 push, which will reuse `dueInstant` the same way this does.
- **Synthesised, not fetched.** Two sine notes with ramped envelopes, built in Web Audio. The shell is precached whole ([ADR-0012](0012-the-app-is-a-precached-shell.md)), so an audio file would be a payload every Device carries for a setting most of them never switch on — and a ramped envelope matters on its own account: a square edge clicks, and a click is the part a tired ear hears as an alarm.
- **Failure is silence.** No Web Audio, a context the browser refuses to resume, a phone on mute: the chime does not sound and nothing else about the app changes. Nothing here is worth failing a tick over.
- **It rides the tick that already runs.** A bottle nears its end by time passing alone, so the ten-second tick that moves every other countdown carries this too, and switching the setting off silences the very next tick. No new timer, no new state, no schema change — for the same reason [ADR-0017](0017-a-bottle-past-its-life-ends-its-own-feed.md) needed none.
- **Every Baby, not just the one on screen.** The bottle running out is running out whichever name is at the top of the timeline.
