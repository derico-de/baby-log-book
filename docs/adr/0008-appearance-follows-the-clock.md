# The appearance follows the clock, and the clock can only ever make it darker

The app has three appearances rather than two — **day**, **night** and **deep night** — and which one is showing is decided by the Device's local wall clock, not by `prefers-color-scheme`. Night runs from 19:00, deep night from 23:00 until the Household's [Day Start](../../CONTEXT.md), and night again until 07:00. The phone's own dark-mode setting still applies between 07:00 and 19:00, but only in one direction: it can darken the app and can never lighten it, so a phone set to light mode is still dark at 22:00 and a phone set permanently to dark mode stays dark at noon. We chose this over the conventional `prefers-color-scheme` swap — one line of CSS, no JavaScript, and what every other app does — because the phone's setting answers a question about the phone, not about the room, and the single moment this app exists for is a parent standing in an unlit bedroom at 3am. A light screen at that moment wakes the baby, the other parent, and the eyes of the person holding both.

## Consequences

- **[Day Start](../../CONTEXT.md) earns a second job, and no `Night Start` setting appears.** The Household has already stated the hour its night ends; deep night simply runs until that hour. This is the same refusal [ADR-0006](0006-targets-are-stated-not-learned.md) made when the Stale Session threshold wanted one — a setting nobody wants to configure is not the answer to a boundary question.
- **The remaining boundaries are literal hours — 19:00, 23:00, 07:00 — and are not configurable.** They are "nobody is having their morning coffee at this hour" figures rather than averages, which is the same shape as the hidden age-banded stale ceiling. Three numbers in one shared module.
- **Deep night is not an inversion, and not a dimmer.** Peak luminance is capped well below white — the brightest ink in deep night sits at `oklch(0.78 …)`, not at 1.0 — while contrast against the ground stays above 10:1. Nothing is greyed; the whole page is simply printed in a quieter register. Shadows are dropped entirely, because a shadow on a near-black ground is only noise.
- **This costs us the no-JavaScript theming that [ADR-0005](0005-claim-links-instead-of-passwords.md)'s framework research prized.** Pico's dark mode is a pure custom-property swap needing no script, but a clock cannot be read from CSS. A small script must resolve the appearance in `<head>` before first paint, or the app flashes the wrong one — and a white flash at 3am is precisely the failure this ADR exists to prevent. It is the only script that may block paint.
- **The appearance changes while the app is open.** A Member watching a Live Session at 22:58 is in night and at 23:02 is in deep night. The resolver re-runs on a timer and on `visibilitychange`; the transition is a plain crossfade of the token values, and is suppressed under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **A manual override stays, and it has three settings, not four.** *Automatic* (the default), *Always day*, *Always night*. There is no *Always deep night* — deep night is a concession to a moment, not a taste, and offering it as a permanent choice would invite someone to run the app at capped luminance in daylight where it fails contrast expectations for the room, not for the ratio.
- **The rule reads the Device's local wall clock, which makes it the second consumer of local time after Day Start** — and [Timezones and travel](../../.scratch/baby-log-book/issues/17-timezones-and-travel.md) has since answered that the two consumers **diverge**, recorded as [ADR-0010](0010-instants-are-stored-the-zone-is-a-lens.md). Days are cut in the Household Zone; the appearance follows whatever clock the Device itself has, because it answers a question about the room rather than about the Household, and it must resolve before first paint with no replica necessarily synced. What crosses between them is the Day Start **hour**, taken here as a plain number: deep night ends at 05:00 on the Device's own clock, wherever that Device is. Getting this one wrong is immediately visible — a white screen in a dark bedroom in Bucharest — rather than quietly wrong in a statistic.
